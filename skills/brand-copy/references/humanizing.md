# Humanizing existing copy

Use this reference for every `brand-copy` task. Apply it while drafting and as a
required final pass, including when the copy already appears natural. Remove
writing tells without flattening the established voice or changing facts.

The pattern catalogue is adapted from the MIT-licensed
[blader/humanizer](https://github.com/blader/humanizer), which builds on
Wikipedia's community-maintained “Signs of AI writing” guidance.

## Preserve before editing

Identify the draft's purpose, audience, facts, voice, structure, and intentional
stylistic choices. Preserve quotations, code, proper names, numbers, and claims
unless the request explicitly includes factual editing.

Treat patterns as evidence in combination, not banned tokens. A single em dash,
formal word, short sentence, or three-item list can be natural. Voice guidance
and deliberate style outrank a generic word list when clarity remains intact.
For interface copy, never change established capitalization, terminology, or
product conventions solely to satisfy this pass.

## Add specificity before personality

Removing bad habits is only half the edit. Sterile prose also feels generated.

- Replace vague reactions with the concrete fact or consequence.
- Let the writer take a position when the source supports one.
- Allow mixed reactions instead of forcing a clean verdict.
- Use first person when the piece is genuinely personal.
- Vary sentence and paragraph length without manufacturing fragments.
- Keep asymmetry when the ideas do not deserve matching sections.
- Do not invent anecdotes, quirks, slang, or opinions to simulate a person.

## High-signal patterns

Prioritize structural problems over isolated vocabulary.

1. **Authority crutches:** “the point is”, “what really matters”, “at its core”, “the real question”. State the claim.
2. **Aphorism formulas:** “X is the Y of Z” metaphors that replace explanation. Use the concrete relationship.
3. **Manufactured punchlines:** stacks of fragments or slogans. Keep the one line that earns emphasis.
4. **Forced symmetry:** automatic triples, matching sections, and repeated sentence shapes. Use the structure the content needs.
5. **Signposting:** “Here's what you need to know”, “This is the answer”, “Let's dive in”. Start with the information.
6. **Negative parallelism:** repeated “not X, but Y” or “not just X” pivots. Make the positive claim directly.
7. **Punctuation tics:** dense em dashes, colons used as dramatic pivots, decorative ellipses. Keep punctuation that clarifies.
8. **Filler and hedging:** automatic “please”, “in order to”, “at this time”, “it is important to note”, or stacked “could potentially”. Cut it or choose the accurate qualifier.
9. **Puffery:** “groundbreaking”, “renowned”, “vibrant”, “pivotal”, “testament”, “breathtaking”. State what happened or what the product does.
10. **Inflated diction and copula avoidance:** “utilize”, “serves as”, “functions as”, “stands as”, “boasts”. Prefer “use”, “is”, or “has” when accurate.
11. **Vague authority:** “experts argue”, “studies show”, “industry reports suggest”. Name and cite the source or remove the claim.
12. **Name-dropping:** lists of people or outlets without explaining their relevance. Keep the source that supports the point.
13. **Dangling participles:** “highlighting”, “showcasing”, “ensuring”, or “reflecting” clauses that add no fact. Delete or make the relationship explicit.
14. **Generic adversity:** “Despite challenges, the project continues to thrive.” Replace with the actual constraint and result.
15. **AI-associated vocabulary clusters:** additionally, crucial, delve, enduring, enhance, foster, garner, interplay, intricate, landscape, pivotal, showcase, tapestry, testament, underscore, vibrant. Replace only when the word is generic rather than precise.
16. **Synonym cycling:** several terms for the same concept in one passage. Choose one and repeat it.
17. **False ranges:** “from X to Y” when the endpoints are not a meaningful scale. List the items directly.
18. **Decorative formatting:** title-case headings, excess bolding, and emojis used as structure. Let hierarchy and prose do the work.
19. **Chatbot residue:** “Of course!”, “Certainly!”, “I hope this helps”, “Let me know if”, or praise before the answer. Remove it from the deliverable.
20. **Generic conclusions:** “The future looks bright.” End with the implication, decision, next step, or earned final image.
21. **Abstract jargon:** substrate, wedge, vector, locus, nexus, primitive, surface, bedrock, scaffolding, modality, paradigm, flywheel. Use the concrete object or action when the abstraction adds nothing.
22. **Unexplained feelings:** “simple”, “intuitive”, or “easy to read” without the mechanism. Show what creates that property.
23. **Dense sentences:** several independent ideas joined into one polished sentence. Split or remove the weaker clause.
24. **Passive default:** missing actors when the actor matters. Name who or what performs the action.
25. **Weak verbs plus adverbs:** “significantly improves”. Give the measured change or choose the stronger accurate verb.

## Editing workflow

1. Read once for meaning and voice before marking patterns.
2. Audit for repeated structures and clusters, not isolated words.
3. Rewrite the smallest span that fixes the problem.
4. Restore specificity, rhythm, or stance where cutting left the prose sterile.
5. Re-read for changed meaning, factual drift, and lost voice.
6. Ask what still makes the result feel generated and fix only supported issues.

For a substantial humanizing review, return concise findings, the revised
draft, and a before-and-after count of the detectable repeated patterns. When
another loaded skill owns the requested review format, fold relevant edits into
that review instead of producing a separate humanizing report. For a narrow
edit, return the rewrite without manufacturing an audit report.

## Final humanizing check

- Did the edit preserve the author's meaning and factual claims?
- Are repeated formulas gone without banning normal punctuation or vocabulary?
- Did vague language become more concrete?
- Does sentence rhythm vary naturally?
- Does the result retain an identifiable voice?
- Was any personality, anecdote, or certainty invented?
